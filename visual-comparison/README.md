# Visual Prompt Interpretation & Comparison Annotation

**Portfolio case study | Feyifunmi Omwade Elizabeth**

## Objective

Demonstrate a repeatable workflow for converting a detailed visual instruction into a clear, factually grounded comparison graphic. The example prompt contrasts a lush rainforest canopy with a barren desert featuring sparse vegetation.

This is a planning and quality-control case study, not a claim of client delivery or a finished photo-based graphic.

## Deliverable concept

A 16:9 horizontal comparison layout with two equal panels:
- **Left — Rainforest canopy:** dense, overlapping green canopy with minimal visible ground.
- **Right — Arid desert:** mostly exposed ground, widely spaced shrubs or small trees, and large visible gaps between plants.

Add restrained labels, a clear divider, and a small legend. Use matched scale and comparable overhead/top-down viewpoints so viewers compare vegetation coverage rather than different camera angles.

## Workflow

1. **Parse the prompt:** identify viewpoint, subject, contrast, labels, and output requirements.
2. **Research candidate assets:** search for high-resolution overhead images; prefer original sources, scientific institutions, government sources, or clearly licensed stock.
3. **Verify suitability:** confirm each image actually shows the requested biome and viewpoint; check captions, context, date, and any location claims.
4. **Check usage rights:** record creator, original URL, license/terms, attribution requirements, download date, and any restrictions. Do not assume that publicly viewable means reusable.
5. **Select and crop:** align horizons/viewpoint where possible, use consistent panel dimensions, and avoid crops that exaggerate differences.
6. **Annotate manually:** add short labels and a simple legend in a layout tool such as Figma or Canva; do not use prohibited generative-fill features.
7. **Quality-check:** verify readability, source accuracy, balanced composition, color/contrast, export dimensions, and file naming.
8. **Document limitations:** state if images are illustrative, if locations are uncertain, or if the comparison is not scientifically controlled.

- `quality_check.md` — completed QA checklist
- `prompt_and_decisions.md` — original prompt, interpretation, and key layout choices

## Factual and editorial safeguards

- Do not label a location or species unless supported by a trustworthy source.
- Avoid presenting one image as representative of every rainforest or desert.
- Do not add statistics or scientific claims unless independently verified and cited.
- Keep the comparison fair: matched viewpoint and similarly sized crops where feasible.
- Preserve original asset attribution and license conditions.
- Do not remove watermarks or bypass access restrictions.
